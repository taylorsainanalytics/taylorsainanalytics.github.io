Proactive Compliance & Budget Intelligence

From a recurring three-week Access-and-Excel reporting process to on-demand analysis and forward-looking budget intelligence.

A colleague ran an established compliance process multiple times each year using an Access database, Excel macros, manual validation, and report-building steps. The process helped the business identify licensed assets approaching expiration, but producing the analysis could take approximately three weeks.

I initially approached the work as a reporting-efficiency problem. During discovery and QA, I found a deeper decision-quality problem: not every usage appearing in the legacy process represented current, commercially relevant use. That led to a broader redesign of the analytical logic behind the report.

01 · BEFORE & AFTER

The dashboard was the endpoint. The real work was redesigning the process.

I mapped the legacy workflow, identified where manual effort and reporting delays accumulated, and redesigned the path from source data to decision-ready insight.

02 · PROCESS DISCOVERY

I was solving a reporting-speed problem—and uncovered a decision-quality problem.

01

Map the current state

Documented the recurring workflow and used process-improvement methods to understand inputs, transformations, reviews, handoffs, and outputs.

02

Investigate the anomalies

Built QA views and examined why retired or disconnected programs could still influence budget outputs.

03

Learn the business rules

Partnered with stakeholders to understand usage types, connection status, licensing conditions, exceptions, supplier scenarios, and cost implications.

04

Redesign for decisions

Shifted the solution from reproducing a legacy report to answering which expiring assets were still relevant and what financial exposure required planning.

03 · BUSINESS LOGIC

Which assets actually belong in the forecast?

The model needed to distinguish meaningful current usage from reporting noise before potential costs could be rolled into the budget view.

07 · WHAT I LEARNED

Automating the old report would have solved only half the problem.

The biggest improvement came from understanding why the report existed and challenging the assumptions behind it. Faster reporting mattered, but the more valuable outcome was a model that better reflected the decisions stakeholders actually needed to make.

This project reinforced my approach to business intelligence: understand the process, validate the logic, then build the interface.
